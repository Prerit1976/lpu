Presenter: Do not explain matrices. The analogy that lands: embedding = "every token gets a GPS coordinate in meaning-space"; attention = "each token asks the others: who matters to me?"; feed-forward = "the token consults its memory of everything it read"; layers = "repeat this 30 to 100 times, each pass a little wiser". On the layer slider, say that the numbers are all this is: more layers and wider vectors = more parameters = more memory for patterns. The ordering game takes 2 minutes and is the retention check.

04 Context window · 8 min

The model's working memory is a fixed-size window.

Everything the model knows about this conversation must fit in the window. When it overflows, the oldest messages fall out. Watch it forget your name.

Why it matters to a teacher: a 30-page syllabus + 40 student answers may not fit a small window. Bigger windows cost more per call, and even big windows recall the middle of a long document worse than the ends.
Presenter: Set the window to 120, introduce, add 3 small talks, ask: it still remembers. Add one long document: the intro falls out and the model asks your name again. Then drag the slider to 600 and ask again: the intro is back inside and it remembers. Point: "memory" in a chat app is not learning, it is the window. Mention that apps like ChatGPT add summaries or retrieval on top to fake a longer memory. Keep cost and the n² note to one sentence each.
