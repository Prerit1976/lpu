01 What is a token? · 10 min

The model never sees words. It sees tokens.

Type anything. The same tokenizer that GPT-4o uses runs right here in your browser and chops the text into coloured pieces. Notice: common words are one token, rare words break into parts, and a space at the start of a word counts.

02 The Indian-language token tax · 6 min

Same sentence. Very different bill.

Tokenizers were trained mostly on English. The same greeting in Kannada, Hindi or Tamil costs roughly twice the tokens on GPT-4o's tokenizer, and far more on older ones. More tokens means more money, slower replies, and a smaller effective context window. Edit any line and watch the bars update live.

Edit the sentences
Task Write one line of feedback you'd give a student, in English and in your mother tongue. Which costs more? Then compare the Roman-script version ("Kanglish"). Is it cheaper? With older tokenizers it was by a wide margin; newer ones have closed the gap.
Presenter: Point out that the romanised row is no longer cheaper on o200k (it was 3× cheaper on GPT-3.5's tokenizer). Discussion prompt: what does this mean for building AI tools for Kannada-medium students? (Answer: pick models with Indic-aware tokenizers, keep prompts short, expect higher cost.)

03 Weightage · Attention · 12 min

Every word asks: who should I pay attention to?

This is the heart of a transformer. Each token sends out a query ("what am I looking for?"), every other token offers a key ("what do I have?"), and the match scores become weightages that sum to 100%. Click any word to see where its attention goes.

Task Click it in sentence A, then switch to sentence B and click it again. Same word, different weightage. Why? Then drag the focus slider to zero: what does the model lose?
Presenter: Weights here are hand-set to mirror what real attention heads learn, so the demo is deterministic. Real models have dozens of heads per layer doing this in parallel, each looking for something different (grammar, reference, position). Mention "attention is all you need" (2017) once, then move on.

04 Picking the next word · 8 min

It's not magic. It's a probability list, one token at a time.

After all that weighing, the model ends with a score for every possible next token. Three dials decide which one gets written: temperature (how adventurous), top-k (how many candidates survive) and top-p (how much probability mass survives). Press Sample repeatedly and watch the sentence grow.

Task Set temperature to 0.05 and sample 5 times: do you ever get a different word? Now set it to 2.0 with top-k = 8. Then find the settings you'd want for (a) grading rubrics, (b) a creative-writing prompt.
Presenter: Candidate lists are hand-authored to look like real model outputs. Core message: "hallucination" is the same mechanism as creativity, just a confident wrong token winning the lottery. Temperature 0 is not "truth mode", it's "most common mode".
