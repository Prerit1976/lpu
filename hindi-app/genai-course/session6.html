Session 06 · 20 min

Retrieval-Augmented Generation

How to make a model answer correctly about your private knowledge: the RAG pipeline, three patterns, and a support-team case study.

1 · The problem

"Can ChatGPT answer questions about our internal APIs?"

Not out of the box. Public models have never seen your product manuals, error codes or processes. Ask anyway and you get a confident guess. Retrieval-Augmented Generation solves exactly this.

RAG=LLM+Search engine+Your private knowledge
Without RAGThe LLM guesses from general training → hallucinations.
With RAGThe LLM first retrieves your documents → grounded answers → far fewer hallucinations.
2 · The pipeline

Four boxes

User query→Retriever→LLM generator→Final answer
RetrieverSearches your company's documents. Documents are split into chunks, each chunk is turned into an embedding (a vector), and stored in a vector database. The query is embedded the same way and the nearest chunks are returned.
LLM generatorReads the retrieved chunks plus your prompt and writes an accurate reply, ideally with citations back to the chunks.

Day-to-day problem it solves

NOC and support teams: debugging, incident response, internal documentation. Nobody remembers every runbook at 3 a.m.

3 · Case study

The on-call runbook assistant

A support engineer pastes an error code; the assistant retrieves the matching runbook sections and proposes the fix with the source quoted. Why this is the ideal first RAG project:

Hallucination checkPublic LLMs do not know your private error codes. Without RAG they guess; with RAG they are accurate.
SpeedSimulates resolving a production outage in seconds rather than minutes.
PrivacyShows how to bring data to the model without retraining or uploading it to a training set.
4 · Patterns

Three RAG patterns, in order of maturity

PatternShapeUse when
Simple RAGRetriever → LLM → answerSingle-turn Q&A over a document set
Conversational RAGChat history + retrieved docs → LLMFollow-up questions need earlier context
Agentic RAGAgent retrieves, validates facts, cross-checks sources, refines, repeatsHigh-stakes answers; multi-step research
Why agentic RAG is the current trendMulti-step retrieval with validation means fewer hallucinations, at the cost of more tokens and latency.
5 · A product you can study today

NotebookLM as a RAG case study

Upload a few documents to NotebookLM and you are watching an enterprise-grade RAG pipeline run:

  • Document grounding: it shows citations for every line, exactly what enterprise RAG should do.
  • Multi-document context: ask across several files at once, classic RAG over a corpus.
  • Chunking and embeddings (hidden): it splits your files into chunks, embeds them and stores them.
  • Semantic retrieval: your question is embedded and matched to the closest chunks.
  • Generation: the LLM writes the final answer from those chunks.
Capstone · 30 minTake three documents from your own work (a policy, a manual, a FAQ). Load them into NotebookLM or a similar tool. Ask five questions you know the answers to and two it cannot possibly answer. Score it: grounded, hallucinated, or correctly declined. Write up what you would need before trusting it with real users.
